Windows will show a warning. That's expected.

When you open the file, Windows shows a blue box saying it doesn't recognise the app. This happens to every program that hasn't been registered with Microsoft, which costs money and is not worth it for a small app like this one. Here's how to get past it:

Windows protected your PC Microsoft Defender SmartScreen prevented an unrecognised app from starting.
  1. Click More info — it's the small grey text in that blue box.
  2. A new button appears at the bottom. Click Run anyway.
  3. The normal installer opens. Click through it as usual.

If you'd rather not, that's completely fine — just ask Rein to install it for you.

On a Mac, it takes a few more steps

Apple blocks apps that haven't been registered with them, and unlike Windows it won't let you click straight through. It's not difficult, just fiddly — here's the whole thing:

  1. Open the downloaded .dmg file and drag Cypher App into your Applications folder.
  2. Open Applications and double-click Cypher App. macOS will refuse and show this:
“Cypher App” Not Opened Apple could not verify “Cypher App” is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy.
  1. Click Done. This step is expected — it has to be refused once before Apple will let you allow it.
  2. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security.
  3. Scroll all the way down to the Security section. You'll see “Cypher App was blocked to protect your Mac.” Click Open Anyway.
  4. Enter your Mac password, then click Open Anyway once more.

You only do this once. After that it opens normally, like any other app.

On older Macs (macOS Sonoma and earlier) it's quicker: right-click the app, choose Open, then Open again. Apple removed that shortcut in newer versions.

You'll also need a key

The app on its own can't read anything. A key is the shared secret that makes your messages match up — think of it as the codebook. Two people can only read each other's messages if they have the exact same key.

Rein will send you a small .json key file. To load it:

  1. Save the file somewhere you'll find it again, like your Downloads folder.
  2. Open Cypher App and click the Key tab at the top.
  3. Click Import Key from File and pick the file you saved.

That's it — you can now encrypt and decrypt messages with whoever shares that key.

Is this safe to install?

It's a personal project written by Rein, not a commercial product. It runs entirely on your own computer: nothing you type is uploaded, and there is no account, no sign-up, and no internet connection required to use it.

Your keys and messages are stored only on your own machine, in your private Windows user folder.

Uninstalling

Open Windows Settings → Apps → find Cypher App → Uninstall. It removes cleanly, the same way any other program does.
